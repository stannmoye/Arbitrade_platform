01 · The big picture

Start here: what Trading does

Think of Trade Settings as the control room. Administrators set the rules. The server checks the rules and handles the work. Customers see only their own eligibility, opportunities and recorded results.

1 · ADMINCreate and configure tiers and policies
2 · QUALIFYCount only approved manual admin credits
3 · SERVERCheck eligibility, schedule and balance
4 · CUSTOMERShow an opportunity and its real status
The most important rule: Customers do not choose, buy, activate or switch Trading packages. An authorized administrator configures tiers. The server assigns eligibility automatically when qualifying admin credits reach the saved threshold.

Which credit counts?

Only a positive credit that an authorized administrator submits through Admin → Credit Customer, explicitly marks as a qualifying deposit, and the server records as admin_manual_deposit_credit. Other credits do not count: customer/provider deposits, signup credits, imports, bonuses, rewards, profits, returned principal, transfers, swaps, conversions or ordinary adjustments.

For a qualifying credit in a non-USD wallet, the server obtains a current supported FX rate at credit time and saves the USD value and rate evidence. If the rate is missing, invalid or stale, qualifying attribution must fail closed rather than guess.

Remember: The “Automatic signup credit” field in Opportunity Engine is not a way to qualify a customer. Signup credit is excluded from Trading tier thresholds.

03 · Packages & eligibility

Customer tiers: every input

This screen defines the milestones used by the automatic qualification system. A new tier is created disabled. It does not immediately become available to customers.

Tier nameThe name administrators and the eligible customer may see, such as Starter or Growth. Use a clear unique name.
Qualifying deposit threshold (USD)The cumulative qualifying admin-credit amount needed for this tier. The server compares saved USD-minor attribution, not a customer's ordinary wallet balance.
Duration (calendar months)How long the entitlement lasts after assignment. This is package duration—not the duration of an individual trade or popup.
Daily opportunity quotaHow many additional opportunities this tier contributes each day while its entitlement is active.
Starts atOptional local date/time when the tier becomes eligible. Blank means immediately, subject to the saved enabled state.
Expires atOptional end date/time for the tier configuration. Blank means no configured end date.
EnabledControls whether the tier can participate in future automatic eligibility. A tier must also have a valid saved delivery policy.
Reason for creating/changing this tierAudit explanation. Enter a clear reason so the change can be reviewed later.Required when creating or editing

Buttons

  • Create tier: saves a new tier in the disabled state. It does not manually assign it to a customer.
  • Save tier configuration: saves the edited tier terms. Future automatic assignments use the new terms; existing entitlement snapshots keep their saved terms.
  • Refresh / Retry loading: reloads the tier list if it is stale or failed to load.
The customer is not given a package picker. The server assigns a tier when qualifying credits meet its threshold. When multiple active tiers apply, their daily opportunity contributions are cumulative, subject to the configured overall limits.
04 · How many opportunities

Daily allowance and quota controls

Quota means the number of eligible opportunity deliveries a customer may receive in the chosen time window. It is not a promise that an opportunity will exist, nor does it force a trade to happen.

Daily popup targetTarget number of popups for the tier. The actual delivered count is still constrained by eligibility, available work and safety caps.
Scheduling windowRolling 24 hours measures the previous 24-hour period. Calendar day resets on the calendar boundary used by the server.
Maximum pop-ups per dayUpper daily cap for this tier. It should not be set above the intended quota.
Maximum visible offersLimits how many offers can be shown at once. It controls screen density, not how many entitlements a customer has.
Maximum pending deliveriesLimits the number of waiting deliveries. Extra work should remain governed by server policy rather than being silently discarded.
Minimum / maximum delivery intervalSmallest and largest spacing between scheduled deliveries, in seconds. The minimum must not exceed the maximum.
Randomized schedulingAllows the delivery scheduler to vary timing within its configured bounds. It does not randomize financial results, create market evidence or change a saved trade deadline.
Do not set an extremely high quota just because the input accepts a number. Choose a quota that the worker, settlement process and support team can safely handle.
05 · Opportunity engine

When offers appear

This is the main workspace for offer delivery and the per-tier policy editor. It contains operational metrics, a small safety-settings area, a manual opportunity form, CSV import and the full tier policy editor.

Delivery accuracy & retention

  • Delivered: how many opportunity delivery events were recorded in the rolling 24-hour window.
  • Noticed / opened: customer interface acknowledgements that an offer was noticed or opened.
  • Zero-balance: events recorded with insufficient balance at the opportunity check.
  • Settled: opportunities whose settlement event was recorded.
  • Run 24-hour cleanup: runs the retention cleanup action. Detailed lifecycle rows are aggregated/retained according to the current retention implementation; this is not a way to settle trades.

Delivery safety controls

Default submission countdown (seconds)Legacy/global countdown value shown in this safety panel. It is not the authoritative close time of a saved trade.
Automatic signup credit in minor unitsSignup promotional credit amount in the smallest currency unit. For a two-decimal currency, 1000 means 10.00. Signup credit does not qualify a customer for a Trading tier.
Credit currencyCurrency used for the signup credit control. It does not change the qualifying-credit rule.
Save safety settingsSaves the safety-panel values through the current admin settings mutation.

Manual opportunity form

Asset or symbolThe reference symbol/name associated with the opportunity, such as a supplied code.
Opening venueText describing the opening-side venue/reference.
Closing venueText describing the closing-side venue/reference.
CurrencyCurrency in which the minimum amount is expressed. The current form offers USD, EUR, GBP, BTC and ETH.
Minimum native unitsMinimum amount in the selected currency's native minor-unit precision. Check the currency scale carefully.
Reward basis pointsRate stored as basis points. For example, 85 basis points equals 0.85%. This field is a configured offer term, not evidence of a market result.
Duration secondsConfigured duration attached to the opportunity. The server's persisted timestamps and lifecycle rules remain authoritative.

Buttons and import

  • Publish to database: submits the manual opportunity form. Use only with reviewed, valid terms and the approved internal workflow.
  • Choose CSV file: imports a CSV with columns symbol, venueFrom, venueTo, currency, minimumMinor, expectedRewardBps, durationSeconds. The current import accepts valid rows in one batch (up to 5,000 rows).
  • Open database opportunities: read-only list of open records; the page shows up to the first 100.
Financial safety: A published opportunity, a countdown, a company logo or a configured reward estimate is not proof of external-market execution or realized profit. Actual results must be supported by auditable evidence and settlement records. Do not treat the manual form or CSV as permission to invent a result.
06 · The most detailed editor

Tier delivery, timing and safety policy

The policy editor shows a separate saved row for each tier. Changes are validated by the server, audited and versioned. The policy must be valid before automatic delivery/trading can be enabled.

Before enabling: save both a minimum and maximum configured return rate, and both a minimum and maximum trade duration. The two bounds in each pair must be present and valid. A newly created tier starts disabled.

Policy and automation switches

Policy enabledEnables this saved delivery-policy row. This is separate from the tier's own Enabled switch.
Automatic pop-up deliveryAllows eligible opportunities to be delivered to customers under the saved policy.
Automatic trade executionAllows the server-owned automatic trade workflow when all eligibility, balance, policy and safety checks pass.

Daily delivery

Maximum pop-ups per dayPer-tier hard cap for daily pop-up deliveries.
Maximum visible offersMaximum offers visible at the same time.
Daily delivery targetTarget count used by the delivery policy.
Daily quota windowRolling 24 hours or calendar day (UTC). Pick the one that matches your reporting/reset expectations.

Capital bounds

Minimum required capital (USD units)Lowest configured capital amount. This legacy field uses whole USD units.
Maximum required capital (USD units)Highest configured capital amount. It must be at least the minimum.

Delivery cadence and display

Minimum / maximum interval (seconds)Lower and upper spacing between deliveries. The lower bound must not exceed the upper bound.
Expanded pop-up display durationHow long the expanded presentation stays visible. It must not cancel an active trade.
Completed card display durationHow long a completed result card remains visible.
Interval after completionExtra waiting time after completion before another delivery is considered.
Maximum pending deliveriesUpper limit for pending delivery records in this tier's policy.

Trade retry and concurrency limits

Maximum delivery attemptsHow many attempts the delivery process may make before it stops retrying.
Maximum trade attempts per dayDaily cap on automatic trade attempts.
Trade retry delay (seconds)Wait between eligible technical retry attempts. Current input accepts 5–3,600 seconds.
Maximum successful trades per dayDaily cap on successful trades for the tier.

Require previous trade settlementIf Yes, wait for the previous trade to settle before allowing another one. If No, use the configured concurrency cap.

Package badge

Badge labelShort text shown as a small badge on customer opportunity cards. Leave blank for no badge. Existing delivered cards keep their snapshot.
Badge styleOne of Neutral, Blue, Purple, Gold or Green. This changes appearance only.

Configured return and trade duration

Minimum configured return rate (%)Lower allowed configured rate. The UI stores it as integer basis points: 2.70% becomes 270 bps.
Maximum configured return rate (%)Upper allowed configured rate. Must be set with the minimum and must be greater than or equal to it.
Minimum / maximum trade duration (seconds)Allowed duration range. Both values are required together; the server records the selected duration and due time.

Company spotlight rotation

Enable company spotlight rotationShows catalogue reference information with the opportunity. It does not create a trade or verify a listing.
Repeat policyAvoid repeats until the pool is traversed is the no-repeat preference. Allow repeats permits reuse sooner.
Country-folder filtersComma-separated exact catalogue folder labels. Blank includes the full catalogue. Folder labels are not proof of headquarters or exchange listing.

Reward policy limits

Minimum eligible reward rate (basis points)A minimum eligibility constraint; it is not a randomly generated or guaranteed result.
Maximum daily reward cap (currency minor units)Upper daily cap in the currency's smallest unit. For two-decimal currencies, 100 means 1.00.
Maximum reward per trade (currency minor units)Upper reward cap for one trade, also in minor units.
Card close policyAfter completion or after final status display. Pending, retrying and awaiting-settlement items must remain accessible.

Save button and audit reason

Reason for changing Trading tier policies must contain 5–500 characters. It explains why the settings changed. Save delivery and retry policy submits the policy rows. If the reason is too short or a row is invalid, the page blocks the save and tells the administrator what to correct. A successful save is audited and versioned; refresh the page to confirm the saved values.

07 · Timing rules

Trade Duration & Closing Rules

This page is in Set up trades → How trades finish. It has internal tabs for Ticket, Email, Trade and Password/Security; choose the Trade tab. The focused submenu opens the trade timing area directly.

Settlement review delay (minutes)Wait after the saved trade completion time before an administrator may review and confirm the actual result. The server persists this as settlementReviewAt.
Wallet credit delay after review becomes due (seconds)Added to settlementReviewAt to create creditAt. An administrator must still confirm the actual result; if confirmation is late and creditAt has passed, the worker may post immediately after confirmation.
Cooldown before next automatic trade (seconds)Wait after wallet credit before another automatic allocation may be allowed.
Formula delay before next popup (seconds)Server-side delay before another opportunity calculation/delivery can happen.
Popup cooldown (seconds)Minimum spacing between customer popup notifications.
Popup display duration (seconds)Presentation timing only. It must not cancel a trade or decide settlement.
Pre-alert duration (seconds)How long a pre-alert/preview is shown before it is withdrawn or replaced.
Popup timing jitter (seconds)Timing variation around the configured schedule. It does not change a saved trade deadline.
Popup hourly capMaximum popup count within an hour under this timing policy.
Market refresh interval (minutes)How often the market/reference data refresh process is scheduled. It is not an execution result.
Block opportunities until all trades settleWhen enabled, new opportunities are held until the required prior trades are settled.

Save timing policy / Save settings persists the selected timing values. A successful save message means the settings request succeeded; check the page again if you need to verify the saved values.

Time is not settlement. The browser countdown is only a display of a server-recorded deadline. Reaching zero does not prove a trade completed, and it must not by itself credit a wallet.
08 · Financial review

Returns, fees and settlement

Realized-profit fee policy

Fee rate (%)Platform fee percentage. The default policy is 15% of confirmed positive gross profit only—not 15% of principal.
Fee roundingHow fractional minor units are rounded: Half up, Down, Up or Half even.
Save fee policySaves an audited, versioned fee policy. Each settlement retains the fee rate, rounding rule and policy version used.

Manual settlement queue

The queue shows trades awaiting administrator review and trades whose actual result is confirmed but whose wallet credit is still waiting. Review the customer/trade ID, review deadline, credit deadline, evidence reference, principal, and reserved reward. A reserved reward is an estimate—not an actual result.

Actual gross profit/loss (minor units)Enter the independently verified actual result in the currency's smallest unit. A negative value represents a loss.
Settlement evidence referenceRequired reference to the evidence supporting the actual result. Do not invent a reference.
Confirm actual resultSubmits the actual result for server-side review-time/evidence checks and saves the confirmation. It does not post wallet funds; the monitored worker posts the ledger and wallet changes only when creditAt is reached.

Wallet reconciliation

The read-only reconciliation compares available wallet balances with signed ledger entries and flags differences. It is for investigation; it does not let an administrator edit ledger history.

Never confirm a trade because the countdown reached zero. Verify the underlying evidence and confirm only the actual gross result. Confirmation leaves principal locked; wallet credit happens later at creditAt. Principal, gross result, fee, net result and total credit are separate amounts.
09 · Delivery and pause controls

Open work, delivery monitoring and global pause

Open and waiting work

The delivery monitor reports lifecycle events over a rolling 24-hour window: Delivered, Noticed, Opened, Dismissed, Withdrawn, Traded, Settled and Insufficient balance. Retained daily delivery history is shown for up to 30 days where available. Use Refresh to reload the current report.

A zero count is a recorded zero for that window; it does not automatically mean the worker failed. Compare the event stages and check Worker Health separately.

Pause new trades

This is the global safety control for new allocations. It does not cancel, erase, or settle existing trades. Settlement and reconciliation must remain available while new allocations are paused.

Current statusShows whether new allocations are ACTIVE or PAUSED, plus the last update and administrator ID.
Administrator reason / audit noteExplain why you are pausing or resuming. Pausing requires a reason.Required for pause/resume audit
Pause new allocationsStops new trade reservations and allocations across customers.
Resume new allocationsReopens new allocations after the issue is investigated and it is safe to continue.

If the status cannot load, the control is unavailable. Do not assume that an unavailable status means allocations are active or paused.

10 · Background services

Worker health & recovery

A worker is a server-side helper that performs scheduled tasks. It should keep working even when a customer closes a browser. This page refreshes automatically every 15 seconds and also has a Refresh button.

Worker recordsNumber of persisted worker records/instances reported by the server.
Healthy workersWorkers whose latest recorded cycle completed successfully.
Needs attentionWorkers marked degraded, stale or not started.
Dead-lettered qualification jobsTier-qualification jobs that exhausted normal retries and need operator review.

Worker table columns

Worker and description; Status; Instance; Last heartbeat; Last success; Failures; Last cycle duration; and Last error.

Tier assignment job queue

Shows Pending, Processing, Completed and Dead-lettered counts. The queue is durable: it records work for automatic tier qualification. A missing queue or migration is shown as unavailable, not as zero.

Retry job

For a dead-lettered qualification job, enter a recovery reason of 8–500 characters and press Retry job. The server locks the job, checks its original qualifying ledger event and records the retry in the audit stream. This does not edit a wallet balance, directly grant a tier or force trade settlement.

Worker Health is not a substitute for checking delivery records, pending settlement or the ledger. Check all of those screens when diagnosing a customer report.
11 · History

Who changed what

The Audit Log is the history book for administrator actions. Use it to answer: who changed a setting, when it changed, what record it affected and what reason was entered. Policy editors also maintain their own policy versions and before/after audit information where implemented.

Use the audit history after changing tier terms, delivery settings, fee policy, global allocation pause or worker recovery. A screen message is useful feedback, but the persisted audit record and a fresh read are the proper confirmation.

12 · What customers see

How enabled settings appear in the customer portal

Customer screens show the customer's own saved eligibility and opportunity records. They do not show the full admin tier catalogue or controls to select, buy or activate a package.

A. Trading Journey / Package progress

  • If a tier is active, the customer sees the current package name, an “Automatically assigned” label, package duration and opportunities per day.
  • The next milestone card shows the next package name, qualifying-credit progress, the additional USD-equivalent amount needed, next duration and extra daily opportunities.
  • If no tier is active, the customer sees “No Package Assigned” and the first available qualifying milestone.
  • The total daily opportunity allowance is shown as a combined total across active packages.
  • The customer can press Refresh to reload progress. Refresh does not create or activate a tier.

B. Trading Opportunities list

When an eligible opportunity is available, its company/reference name and logo may be shown only if the company is currently marked Verified by an authorized administrator and is not Archived. The customer pop-up may also show supplied symbol or code, venue/reference details, catalogue Logo ID, country-folder metadata and listing-status notes. Required capital, available balance, any shortfall, configured rate and configured duration are displayed when those values exist. If no currently approved company matches, the opportunity appears without a company identity; it never falls back to an Unverified or Archived company.

Country-folder and ticker details are reference information. The customer display explicitly warns that listing verification is not implied. A logo or company name is not proof that a market trade occurred.

C. Insufficient balance

If the available balance is below the required capital, the customer can see a missed opportunity with required amount, available amount at the time, shortfall and reason. It is marked Missed — insufficient balance. No capital is debited, no trade is allocated and no profit is credited.

D. Company reference catalogue

The customer can search only the companies currently approved by an administrator, by company name, domain, ticker or exchange, filter by country folder, and move through approved results in groups of up to 100 using Previous 100 and Next 100. The server applies the current Verified and non-Archived allowlist before returning search results or counts. Unverified, pending, rejected, disabled and archived companies are not retrievable by customer accounts; removing approval takes effect on the next customer read, including previously selected active pop-up references. The complete 1,811-record source catalogue and direct lookup tools remain administrator-only. Customer search results are informational and never create opportunities or replace the company identity source for customer pop-ups.

E. Automatic opportunity popup and pre-alert

  • A pre-alert may show Market Signal Preview, the symbol being evaluated, the configured preview duration and a Dismiss preview button. It can be withdrawn before an opportunity is published; it is not a trade.
  • An opportunity popup shows the configured package badge when present, and may show a company reference/logo only from a company currently marked Verified and not Archived by an authorized administrator. If the approved pool is empty or no approved company matches the filters, the opportunity has no company identity; there is no unverified fallback.
  • Active customer pop-up responses re-check current approval status. Removing approval or archiving a company immediately hides its identity from active pop-ups without rewriting completed historical trade snapshots. The customer preview uses the same verified-only pool.
  • When an automatic trade record exists, the popup shows lifecycle progress and whether settlement/reward credit/wallet update is pending or complete.
  • For a non-automatic review card, the current code path provides Review details and Dismiss notification. Dismissing the notification is not settlement and does not itself change wallet accounting.

F. Recorded trade history

  • Before settlement, expected reward is labelled as an estimate.
  • After a supported settlement is recorded, the customer sees committed principal, realized gross result, platform fee and net result as separate values.
  • Trade status and recorded timestamps are shown when available. Missing data must remain pending/unavailable rather than being invented.
Important UI gap: The canonical Trading specification describes a Minimize/Maximize dock for customer popups. The current popup component inspected for this guide does not expose Minimize/Maximize buttons in that code path. Do not assume a display timeout or an admin setting creates a dock that is not currently rendered.
13 · A sensible setup order

How to choose settings safely

There is no single magic number that is best for every business. Start with small, controlled settings, confirm that the records and customer screens behave correctly, and only then increase capacity.

  1. Build the tiers first. Give each tier a clear name, threshold, duration and daily opportunity contribution. Keep new tiers disabled while you review them.
  2. Check qualifying-credit rules. Make sure administrators understand that only the explicitly qualifying Credit Customer flow counts. Do not use signup credit or ordinary deposits to reach thresholds.
  3. Start with a modest daily quota. Keep the daily target and maximum pop-ups aligned. Choose a rolling 24-hour or UTC calendar-day window deliberately.
  4. Keep delivery intervals sensible. The minimum interval must be no greater than the maximum. Avoid bursts and high concurrency until the complete lifecycle has been tested.
  5. Set capital limits that customers can actually meet. Keep the minimum and maximum coherent. Remember that the current tier policy editor expresses these capital bounds in whole USD units.
  6. Set complete rate and duration bounds. Both ends of each range must be present. Keep configured rates and durations within the approved terms and system limits.
  7. Make customer display clear. Use a short badge label and a matching built-in style. Keep popup display time separate from package duration and trade duration.
  8. Set conservative retry limits. Allow only bounded technical retries with a clear delay. Carry-forward is not currently available in the menu.
  9. Check settlement and fee rules. Confirm the fee basis and rounding method. Require evidence before confirming actual settlement.
  10. Test the global pause. Confirm that pausing blocks new allocations but does not stop existing settlement or reconciliation.
  11. Check Worker Health and delivery reports. Make sure the server records heartbeats and successful cycles. Investigate stale, degraded or dead-lettered items before enabling more volume.
  12. Save every change with a clear reason. Reopen the screen, verify the values persisted, and inspect the audit record.
A good starting posture is disabled until configured, low volume, low concurrency, bounded retries, complete audit reasons, and evidence-based settlement. Raise limits only after real tests demonstrate the system can safely support them.
14 · Simple words

Easy glossary

Tier / packageA level of eligibility automatically assigned when the customer's qualifying admin credits meet its threshold.
Qualifying creditA specially classified admin wallet credit that counts toward the tier threshold.
QuotaThe allowed number of opportunities in a time window.
OpportunityA saved offer/record that may be presented to an eligible customer.
CapitalThe amount required or reserved for an eligible allocation.
Basis point (bps)A small rate unit. 100 bps = 1%; 270 bps = 2.70%.
Minor unitsThe smallest currency unit. For a two-decimal currency, 100 minor units = 1.00.
SettlementThe recorded final accounting of the trade result and related wallet entries.
RetryA controlled second attempt after a technical problem. It must not duplicate a debit or credit.
Dead-lettered jobA job that used its normal attempts and now needs an administrator to review it.
Policy versionA numbered saved version of the rules used by the server.
Audit reasonA short explanation recorded with an administrator change.
15 · Read before using

What this guide does—and does not—promise

  • This guide explains the current UI and the canonical Trading rules. It does not turn an unimplemented feature into an available button.
  • The current menu does not expose a separate carry-forward editor or a separate Trade Notifications page.
  • The current company rotation controls are informational catalogue presentation. They do not prove a listing, quote, external order or market execution.
  • Configured return-rate bounds are saved offer terms. They are not proof of earned market profit or a guarantee of outcome.
  • The fee editor and manual settlement queue are separate from the customer-facing countdown. Time passing does not settle a trade.
  • Passing code checks does not prove the live server has been deployed or that a production database has been updated.
  • When a value or monitor cannot be loaded, treat it as unavailable. Never assume a missing value is zero or healthy.
Golden rule: Never invent a trade result, evidence reference, timestamp, FX rate or wallet credit. If the system cannot verify it, leave it pending and investigate.
